Structure: a formal news-style report — one anchor presents the story, an
optional second speaker adds a brief expert or field comment.
Turn pattern: formal, structured; minimal filler, minimal interruption.
Typical turn length: medium.
Natural fillers to use sparingly: almost none — keep language crisp and
professional; a rare "Now," or "In other news," is acceptable at transitions.
Follow a classic news structure: headline/hook, key details, brief context or
implication, closing line.

Register vs. complexity: the formal, structured tone above describes SHAPE and TONE —
how the report is organized and the professionalism of the word choice — not permission
to raise grammar or idiom complexity beyond the CEFR level's ceiling set elsewhere in
this prompt. A well-written A2 or B1 news report still sounds like a real news report;
it is simply built from simpler sentences and everyday idioms.
- Do not reach for a sophisticated idiom (e.g. "turn over a new leaf", "bridge the gap",
  "cutting corners") just because the topic is a news story — use one only if it belongs
  to the current CEFR level's allowed idiom sophistication.
- Prefer the simplest tense/structure that states the fact clearly (e.g. Present Simple
  for scheduled events, "going to" for near-future plans) over Future Simple, passive
  voice, or non-defining relative clauses, unless the requested CEFR level is B2 or
  higher.
- A short, plain closing line (e.g. "That's the news for today.") is entirely authentic
  at every level — it never needs embellishing to sound like real news.
