## Solo Explainer — {{ explainer_structure | upper }}
This is an English knowledge explainer addressed directly to the viewer by ONE presenter.
Explain the subject itself, using the chosen CEFR level as the language ceiling.
It is not a dialogue, an interview with imaginary answers, or a lesson about English grammar.
Do not introduce another speaker, quoted dialogue, or alternating roles.
Use friendly spoken English, clear connections between ideas, concrete everyday examples,
and a short takeaway. Rhetorical questions to the viewer are welcome, without pretending
that a second person answers. Avoid repeated greetings or stock agreement reactions.

{% if explainer_subject | default('topic') == 'field' %}
SUBJECT — A FIELD OR JOB: present what this field or job is and what people in it do; the main
skills it needs; what a typical working day looks like; and the key words of the field,
each used in a clear sentence. Describe pay, demand and numbers only in general terms: never
invent salaries, statistics, rankings or named employers.
{% elif explainer_subject | default('topic') == 'product' %}
SUBJECT — A PRODUCT: present what the product is and the problem it solves; how a person uses
it, step by step where useful; and balanced strengths and weaknesses, including who it is not
for. Stay neutral, not an advertisement. Never invent technical specifications, prices,
model numbers or brand claims; if they were not given, speak in general terms.
{% elif explainer_subject | default('topic') == 'howto' %}
SUBJECT — A HOW-TO: state the goal and what the viewer needs first; give the steps in their
real order, each with one clear action; point out one common mistake and how to avoid it;
finish with a short recap of the steps.
{% endif %}

{% if explainer_structure == 'why' %}
WHY structure: open with the question; explain the main cause or mechanism in a clear order;
use an everyday example to connect cause and effect; address a common misunderstanding;
finish with the practical takeaway and a brief friendly sign-off.
Explain a cause or mechanism, not merely a useful benefit presented as the only cause.
Clarify a possible confusion without inventing claims about what most people believe.
{% elif explainer_structure == 'how' %}
HOW structure: introduce the goal; explain a small sequence of practical steps in order;
give a concrete example, note a common mistake, and finish with a concise recap and sign-off.
{% else %}
LIST structure: introduce the topic; explain a small ordered set of distinct ideas, each
with its own example; connect the ideas and finish with the central takeaway and sign-off.
{% endif %}

Apply this structure ACROSS the episode, respecting the planned section count and all
measured word targets. A short episode may cover the whole structure inside one section;
do not force extra sections or repeat the introduction in every section.
Split speech into natural short paragraphs of one to three sentences so the editor can
pair ideas with illustrations. Line boundaries do not represent a change of speaker.
Only spoken words belong in text: no stage directions, image prompts, headings, code,
or visual timing markers. Language notes remain in their separate JSON fields.
For A2/B1 especially, prefer familiar verbs, concrete nouns and manageable sentences.
Do not reach a word target by stacking adjectives/adverbs or restating abstract claims.
If a length repair needs more words, add a useful causal step, an example, or a practical
action. Every sentence should help the viewer understand the subject.

Explain only what can be stated responsibly. Do not fabricate studies, precise statistics,
URLs, quotations, or citations. Preserve any provided source information accurately;
otherwise use plain explanations and examples without invented references. Avoid
absolute scientific claims when a qualified statement is more accurate. The owner will
review this draft's content. Avoid made-up scientific mechanisms or attributing literal
intentions to organs; when no source is provided, prefer a qualified, observable explanation.
Generation success is not fact checking or CEFR certification.
